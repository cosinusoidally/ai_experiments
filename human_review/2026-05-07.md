# 2026-05-07 notes and observations

Today I was getting Codex to try and calculate the values of the tokens I have
used through my Plus plan (see [../process_transcript/calculate_gpt54_session_cost.js](../process_transcript/calculate_gpt54_session_cost.js))
According to that script it's about $300 dollars of usage in the first month.
This got me thinking a bit about what is the true cost of AI in terms of
hardware resources/power consumption. Obviously that $300 won't be the true
cost (they will be attempting to make a profit somewhere) but they must be
subsidising personal accounts to a massive extent (if the above figure is to
be believed then it may be 60x subsidy). If/when open weight models become as
good as GPT-5.4 (which they might actually be) then it just becomes a hardware
cost/running cost equation. ie how many customers can you serve with each
(say $5k, $20k, $100k, whatever) server? If local inference becomes viable on
(relavively cheap) desktops/laptops/mobile phones then the bottom will probably
fall out from openai and Anthropic.

I also tried to get Codex to write a more
[literate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literate_programming)
style version of the script. It largely failed, but in a strange way. I was
asking it to impress Donald Knuth. It could say in great detail what would
impress him, but it just couldn't do it. It kept falling back to writing heavily
commented source code. Or sometimes not even that, it would comment for a bit
and then give up. Even GPT-5.5 xhigh couldn't do a decent job. It's difficult
to build a mental model of what it's doing and why it's failing. There's also
the issue that I can't debug it's reasoning since openai hides the "reasoning"
tokens in the logs. Maybe I need to figure out if I can fix this. Looking at
the docs maybe it is model_reasoning_summary? Just checked and
model_reasoning_summary does help. The reasoning summary (apparently "auto" is
the correct setting, but I might also try "detailed"). The really strange thing
is how the model seems to plateau on this task. The even stranger thing is how
it can very clearly articulate what Donald Knuth would want (and even makes
accurate observations about the current issues with the structure) but it
just can't actually get the program in to a literate style. It's like it is all
mouth and no trousers.
